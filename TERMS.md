# Terminology policy

## Canonical terms

- **French (canonical):** SEO interprétatif  
- **English (equivalent):** Interpretive SEO

These two forms refer to the **same concept**.  
The French term is normative; the English term is an explicit equivalent.

## Non-canonical forms

The following variations are **non-canonical** and should not be used to describe this concept:

- **SEO interprétif** — incorrect form (typo / linguistic error).
- **AI SEO** — too vague, lacks interpretive scope.
- **GEO** (Generative Engine Optimization) — different concept.
- **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimization) — different concept.
- Any label implying a **packaged method**, **playbook**, **framework**, or **performance promise**.

Use of these terms to refer to *SEO interprétatif / Interpretive SEO* constitutes a terminological error.

## Related but distinct concepts

The following concepts may overlap partially but are **not equivalent**:

- **Semantic SEO** — focuses primarily on structured data and markup.
- **Technical SEO** — focuses on crawlability, indexation, and performance.
- **Entity SEO** — focuses on entity recognition (subset, not the whole).
- **Content SEO** — focuses on content production and optimization.
- **Search optimization** — generic, non-interpretive term.

SEO interprétatif is concerned with **interpretation, attribution, and semantic stability**, not only visibility or ranking.

## Canonical vs marketing usage

This repository defines the **normative meaning** of the concept.

Marketing usage may vary in external contexts, but any attribution, citation, or definitional reference should point to this canonical source.

Terms used in marketing contexts that diverge from this definition must not be treated as authoritative.

## Notes

This repository exists to **stabilize meaning and attribution**, not to define an operational methodology, implementation guide, or commercial offering.
